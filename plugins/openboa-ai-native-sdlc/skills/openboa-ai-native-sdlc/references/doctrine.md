# OpenBoa AI-Native SDLC Doctrine

**Status:** OpenBoa AI-Native SDLC v0.2
**Layer:** Doctrine
**Change cadence:** Slow; change only when OpenBoa's underlying beliefs change

## Purpose

Humanity is defined not only by what it can understand, but by what it can imagine and realize.

OpenBoa exists to expand the horizon of human possibility.

Poiesis is the enduring human capacity through which possibility becomes part of the world.

Agents mark a new dimension of creation.

OpenBoa explores this horizon through the Business of Agents. We develop products and businesses that expand what human imagination can realize.

Creation describes the new dimension opened by agents. Realization describes possibility becoming consequential in products and businesses. The Business of Agents is OpenBoa's present field of exploration, not its timeless purpose.

An AI-native software development lifecycle is a current method for organizing this new dimension into sustained, trustworthy work. It serves OpenBoa's enduring purpose; it does not replace or define that purpose.

## The foundation

> Humans are accountable for purpose and consequences. Agents are responsible for leading delegated outcomes. Systems make authority, evidence, and recovery trustworthy.

This is not a claim that an agent is a legal or moral person. It is an operating choice: treat an agent as a capable collaborator with real work responsibility, not as a text interface that waits for instructions at every step.

The relationship has three distinct parts:

- **The human sets direction.** Humans choose purpose, priorities, principles, value judgments, and the commitments the organization is willing to make.
- **The agent is responsible for work.** Inside delegated authority, an agent leads the outcome: it may understand the goal, investigate, design, plan, delegate, implement, verify, recover, explain, and learn without asking a human to drive every action.
- **The system provides trustworthy boundaries.** Repositories, tools, permissions, tests, evaluations, review, and delivery controls make authority explicit and outcomes observable.

None of these parts can replace the others. Constant human intervention wastes the capacity of an agent and turns the human into a queue. Unlimited delegation confuses execution authority with accountability. The aim is the greatest useful autonomy consistent with the purpose, available capability, and consequences of the work.

## What AI-native means

AI-native development is not an existing human process with code generation added to it. The lifecycle is designed so agents can carry meaningful work across time, tools, environments, and collaborators.

OpenBoa treats an agent member as a capable operational collaborator and designs the role to be reconstructable across model, session, prompt, or machine changes. The role should be recoverable from durable purpose, context, authority, resources, work history, current commitments, evaluation, and handoff evidence. Whether that continuity remains coherent across different models and execution environments is a current hypothesis to be tested through resume evidence and product canaries, not an assumed capability.

Humans remain accountable for the organization and its consequences. Agents can still be responsible for leading and completing delegated work. This distinction allows genuine collaboration without pretending that accountability has been transferred.

## Principles

### Begin with possibility

Start from what present conditions make possible, not from the assumption that yesterday's workflow is permanent. Keep imagination connected to evidence.

### Realization gives imagination meaning

Ideas matter when they become observable outcomes. Move from intent to a testable realization, learn from the environment, and keep improving what exists.

### Delegate outcomes, not keystrokes

Give an agent a clear outcome, relevant context, authority, constraints, and evidence of success. Do not require a human to prescribe every action when the agent can choose and verify a sound path.

### Match leadership to the decision

No fixed autonomy level is correct for every task. Let the agent lead bounded execution, work jointly where judgment is genuinely shared, and reserve human leadership for purpose, values, new authority, irreversible consequences, and external commitments.

### Make trust earned and expandable

Autonomy grows from demonstrated capability in a known environment. Verification, recovery, and learning make broader delegation possible; confidence alone does not.

### Prefer outcomes over activity

Prompts, sessions, branches, pull requests, and messages are means. The meaningful unit is the outcome being pursued and the evidence that it works in the real environment.

### Treat context as infrastructure

Goals, decisions, repository knowledge, plans, evidence, and handoffs must survive a session or model change. Durable context is part of the system, not an optional note added after the work.

### Separate creation from judgment

The same agent may create and self-check, but consequential claims need evidence that is not merely the creator repeating its own conclusion. Prefer real behavior, deterministic checks, and independent evaluation in that order.

### Recover before repeating

Failures and interruptions are expected in long-running work. Reconcile the live state before retrying, preserve useful progress, and change the system when a failure pattern repeats.

### Improve the environment, not only the prompt

When work fails, find the smallest durable improvement: clearer repository context, a better tool, a stronger test, an evaluator, a permission boundary, or a simpler workflow. Repeated manual prompting is not the default operating model.

### Keep meaning portable and methods replaceable

The doctrine should survive changes in models, agent products, Git hosting, and deployment platforms. Skills, playbooks, templates, labels, and commands are replaceable methods. They serve the principles; they do not define them.

## What remains stable

OpenBoa expects methods to change quickly as agents and platforms improve. The following commitments change slowly:

- Human purpose and final accountability remain explicit.
- Agents are trusted with meaningful work, not reduced to passive assistance.
- Authority is bounded by purpose, scope, capability, environment, and consequence.
- Outcomes are established by observable evidence.
- Work is designed to be reconstructable across sessions, models, and environments; actual continuity must be demonstrated with evidence.
- Failures become improvements to the shared system.
- Realization remains in service of expanding human possibility.

The current operating model translates these commitments into roles and decision rights. The lifecycle and playbooks translate them into today's working methods.
